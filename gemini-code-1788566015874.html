Solving Equations with Variables on Both Sides: Complete Mastery Guide

Step-by-step mathematical procedures for simplifying expressions, collecting variables, clearing fractions and decimals, and setting up multi-stage word problems.

STRATEGY 1 Simplify Each Side Independently

Always expand parentheses using the Distributive Property and combine like terms on each side of the equation before attempting to move terms across the equal sign:

Demonstration: Solve \( 5(n - 7) = 2(n + 14) \)
• Expand left side: \( 5n - 35 \)
• Expand right side: \( 2n + 28 \)
• Collect variable terms: \( 5n - 2n = 28 + 35 \implies 3n = 63 \implies n = 21 \)

STRATEGY 2 Clearing Fractions and Decimals

Eliminate fractions by multiplying every term on both sides by the Least Common Denominator (LCD), or eliminate decimals by multiplying by powers of 10 (\(10, 100, 1000\)):

Fraction Clearing: Solve \( \frac{q + 1}{2} = \frac{q - 1}{3} \)
• Multiply both sides by \( \text{LCD} = 6 \): \( 3(q + 1) = 2(q - 1) \implies 3q + 3 = 2q - 2 \implies q = -5 \)

Decimal Clearing: Solve \( 0.25t = 0.25 - t \)
• Add \( t \) to both sides: \( 1.25t = 0.25 \implies t = \frac{0.25}{1.25} = 0.2 \) (or \( \frac{1}{5} \))

STRATEGY 3 Special Solutions: Identities vs. Contradictions

  • Identity (Infinitely Many Solutions): Occurs when variable terms cancel out, leaving a universally true numerical statement (e.g. \( 2c + 3 = 2c + 3 \implies 3 = 3 \)). Any real number is a valid solution.
  • Contradiction (No Solution): Occurs when variable terms cancel out, leaving a false numerical statement (e.g. \( 12b + 9 = 12b + 11 \implies 9 = 11 \)). No real number satisfies the equation.

STRATEGY 4 Multi-Step Word Problem Modeling Blueprint

When modeling word problems with variables on both sides, structure your approach through explicit sequential steps:

  1. Define the Variable & Normalize Units: Identify the unknown quantity and ensure all rates, distances, and times share identical units (e.g., converting feet to inches, or minutes to seconds).
  2. Construct the Expressions: Write algebraic expressions for both competing options, moving objects, or account values.
  3. Equate and Solve: Set the two expressions equal to find the break-even point or intersection condition.
  4. Interpret Real-World Constraints: Calculate secondary questions such as final costs, heights, or elapsed rest periods.
